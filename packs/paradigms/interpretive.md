# Interpretive Pack

Claims established by an **argued reading of sources, texts or accounts**. There is no
control group and no p-value; rigour comes from the quality of evidence, the transparency
of the reading, and engagement with rival interpretations.

**Fields:** history, literary studies, philosophy, anthropology, qualitative sociology,
media and cultural studies, science and technology studies, qualitative health research,
legal scholarship.

**`lit-search` domains:** `humanities`, `social`, `edu`

## Question frame

**Corpus / Method of reading / Claim / Rival interpretations / Stakes.**

State it as: *"Reading [corpus] through [method/theoretical lens], what does [claim] show
that [prevailing interpretation] misses, and why does it matter?"*

**The rival-interpretation clause is the rigour.** An argument that never states what it
is arguing against is not a contribution, it is a description.

## Unit of contribution

A reinterpretation supported by evidence, a newly recovered or newly accessible source, a
conceptual distinction that clarifies a confused debate, a critique showing an accepted
reading depends on an unexamined assumption, or a synthesis connecting separate literatures.

## Generation axes

| Axis | Ask |
|---|---|
| Archive | What source, collection or record group has never been read for this question? |
| Voice | Whose account is missing from the standard narrative? |
| Recontextualisation | What does this text or event look like from a different framework? |
| Genealogy | How did this concept come to seem natural, and what did it displace? |
| Comparison | What does the case look like beside a case nobody has compared it to? |
| Contradiction | Where do two established readings conflict, and what resolves it? |
| Periodisation | Does the conventional boundary conceal a continuity or a rupture? |
| Assumption critique | What does the field take as given that a source contradicts? |
| Newly accessible | What has digitisation, declassification or translation just made readable? |
| Method transfer | What analytic approach from a neighbouring field applies here? |

**Newly accessible sources are the strongest and most time-sensitive axis** — digitisation
and declassification create real windows.

## Data and artifacts

Archives and manuscript collections, published texts and editions, interview transcripts,
fieldnotes, oral histories, images and film, legal and government records, digitised
corpora, newspapers, ephemera.

## Tier B — obtainable without new collection

- Digitised archives and national library collections; HathiTrust, Internet Archive,
  Europeana, Gallica, national digital archives
- Newspaper and periodical databases; declassified document collections
- Qualitative data archives with secondary-analysis access (UK Data Service, ICPSR
  qualitative holdings)
- Published corpora, editions and translations
- Court records, legislative proceedings, government open records

**Language competence and archival access are the binding constraints far more often than
existence.** A source that exists in a language the researcher does not read, or an
archive requiring travel and permission, is Tier C — and should be tiered honestly rather
than counted as available.

## Validity threats

1. **Cherry-picked evidence** — quotations selected to fit, counter-evidence unaddressed
2. **Anachronism** — reading present categories into a past that did not have them
3. **Unrepresentative corpus** — what survives is not what existed; survival is biased
4. **Over-reading** — a claim the source cannot bear
5. **Unengaged rival interpretations** — the strongest opposing reading is not addressed
6. **Circularity** — the theoretical framework guarantees the conclusion
7. **Provenance and authenticity** unexamined
8. **Translation and transcription** slippage carrying the argument
9. **Unstated positionality** where the researcher's position shapes access and reading
10. **Insufficient saturation** in interview-based work — too few accounts, too narrow

## Impact anchors

Whether it revises an accepted account, how central that account is to the field, whether
it recovers a source or voice now available to others, its bearing on a live public or
policy debate, its use in teaching and curriculum, and whether it opens a research
programme rather than closing a question.

Citation counts are slow and unrepresentative here; book reviews, adoption in syllabi and
engagement in public debate carry more signal.

## Novelty conventions

Applying a fashionable framework to one more case is weak. Strong novelty: a source nobody
has used, a reading that makes better sense of evidence the standard account struggles
with, a conceptual distinction that dissolves a long-running dispute, or a comparison that
reframes both cases. **Novelty checking must include books, edited volumes, dissertations
and non-English scholarship** — the article-indexed databases behind `lit-search` cover
this pack far worse than they cover the sciences, so state that limitation explicitly
every time.

## What good looks like

- The strongest rival reading is stated in its own best form and then engaged
- Counter-evidence is presented, not only evidence that fits
- The corpus boundary is justified and its survival bias acknowledged
- Categories are the sources' own, or the anachronism is deliberate and flagged
- Provenance is examined where authenticity carries weight
- Positionality is stated where it shapes access or reading
- The argument would still hold if one favourite quotation were removed
- Sources are left more usable to others than they were before

## What this pack cannot see

- **Whether the reading persuades specialists.** Judgement here is expert judgement and
  does not reduce to criteria.
- **Language and palaeographic competence** required for the sources.
- **Archive access** - permission, travel, cataloguing state, and how long each takes.
- **The non-English and book-length scholarship** that carries much of this pack's field.
  The article-indexed databases behind `lit-search` cover it badly, and any novelty claim
  made here has to say so.
